# Welcome to Bookfish

*The greatest book ever written about this app. Also the only one.*

## 1. Hello

Hello. I am Finn Bookfish. I am a fish, and I live for books. Most fish live for worms. I have better taste.

I keep three kinds of books in one place.

E-books are for your eyes. Audiobooks are for your ears. And readalouds are for both at once. A readaloud is an e-book that knows exactly which sentence is being spoken. This little book is one. So it can talk. But first, we read.

Whatever you do, I never lose your place. I have the memory of an elephant, which is unusual for a fish.

**Try it.** Turn the page. Tap the right edge of the screen, or swipe. If that works, you are ready for chapter two.

## 2. Reading

All my controls follow one rule. I am very proud of it. Here it comes.

A tap turns the page or starts playback. A long press opens another view.

That is all. Tap the left or right edge of the page to turn it. You can also swipe. Tap a sentence in the middle of the page, and I read it aloud. Tap the sentence I am speaking, and I pause.

A long press means: press and hold. It shows you something on its own page. A picture. A footnote. A table. And here is the secret code: blue means "press and hold me". Whenever you see blue text, hold your thumb on it and wait for the miracle.

Let us practise. Scientists agree that fish who read are happier than fish who do not.[FOOTNOTE MARK: 1] Do you see the little blue number? Press and hold it. The note opens on its own page, and it tells you how to come back.

Next, a picture. This is a portrait of me on a good day.

Press and hold the picture. It opens on its own page. Pinch it or double-tap it to zoom, and turn the phone if you like. If I am reading aloud, I simply keep reading.

## 3. Finding your way

A book is a big place. Here is how you never get lost.

Look at the page number. If the book knows the pages of its printed edition, I show those. If not, I estimate them, and I put a small wavy equals sign in front. It means "about". Here is an example.

[NOT NARRATED]
```
≈143/380
```
[/NOT NARRATED]

That is page 143 of about 380. I am an honest fish. And the page numbers stay the same when you change the text size.

Now find the page number. It sits at the right end of my button bar. That is the row of small buttons at the top or the bottom of the screen. If you hold the phone sideways, the page number stands at the edge of the page, turned on its side.

No page number? It is hidden while my playback buttons are on the screen. Pause me and turn the page, and it comes back.

Tap the page number. This opens my flip view: nine small pages, with yours in the middle. Swipe to move through the book. Or tap beside the pages. Or drag the bar below them, and fly from the first page to the last. Tap a page to read on there. Close the view, and you are back where you were. "Go to page" lets you type a page number.

Everything else is in the pull-down menu. Pull the page down from the top and choose.

"Contents" lists the chapters.

"Find and history" searches the book. Type a few words, in any order, and I show every paragraph that contains all of them. Type nothing, and the same page shows your history: the places where you have read and listened. Tap an entry to go there.

"Bookmarks" lists your bookmarks and notes. To make one, press and hold a word. Buttons appear. One makes a bookmark. One makes a note. Not sure which button is which? Hold one down, and it shows you its name.

**Try it.** Three steps. If I am reading aloud, pause me and turn the page first. One: tap the page number, look around, and close the flip view again. Two: pull down the menu, choose "Find and history" and search for the word "goldfish". Three: press and hold any word on this page and set a bookmark. I suggest the word "glory".

## 4. Words

Some words need a closer look. Press and hold any word, anywhere on the page. This selects the word, and two handles appear. Drag the handles to select more.

Buttons appear too. Copy the word. Bookmark it. Write a note about it. Or look it up in the dictionary.

The dictionary translates a word or a whole phrase, right on your phone, without any network. It even works at the bottom of the sea. It also knows English words in their other forms. Select "went", and it finds "go".

There is one problem. The dictionaries come from dict.cc. They are free for personal use. But dict.cc does not allow me to pass them on. So I come without a dictionary, and you get your own. It takes four steps.

One. Open the dictionary settings: pull down the menu, choose "Settings", then "Dictionary". If no dictionary is on the phone, you find a link to dict.cc there.

Two. On the dict.cc page, request the dictionary for your pair of languages.

Three. Put the file you get into the Documents folder of your phone.

Four. Go back to the dictionary settings. The file is listed there. Tap "Import" and wait a moment.

Done. You may delete the file afterwards. I have swallowed everything I need.

You can keep several dictionaries, one for each pair of languages. For each further one, visit dict.cc in your browser and repeat steps two to four. The language of the book decides which dictionary I use. So for this book, you need one with English in it.

**Try it.** Press and hold the word "miracle". Drag a handle and watch the selection grow. Then tap the copy button.

## 5. Audiobooks

Now rest your eyes. An audiobook has no pages, so it opens in my player.

The player shows the chapter, your place in it, and the time left in the book. Buttons take you one chapter back or forward, or thirty seconds back or forward. Did you miss a sentence because somebody talked to you? Go back thirty seconds. Then look at that person, slowly.

In the pull-down menu of the player, "Contents" lists the chapters with their start times. "History" shows where you have listened.

Play, pause and the thirty-second jumps also work from the lock screen and from your headphones.

The sleep timer is for listening in bed. It stops me after 15, 30, 45, 60 or 90 minutes, or at the end of the chapter. Before I stop, I slowly get quieter, so I do not wake you.

And I keep your place. While I play, I save it every twenty seconds, and again whenever playback stops. This also works when the phone stops me: for a call, for an alarm, or when your headphones are too far away. Every five minutes, I also write your place into the history. So if you fall asleep in chapter three and wake up in chapter nine, the history takes you back.

**Try it.** This book has no audiobook, but the sleep timer works for readalouds too. Tap a sentence, so that I read aloud. A small clock appears in the button bar. Tap the clock, and choose "15 min" under "Sleep timer". The button now shows the minutes left. Tap it again and choose "Off". Or do nothing, and I stop in fifteen minutes.

## 6. Readalouds

Now both at once. A readaloud is an e-book with the audiobook built in. Every sentence of the text is matched with the same sentence in the recording. So I can mark each sentence while it plays.

So read on the sofa. When the dishes call, tap a sentence. Your hands wash the dishes, and your ears stay in the story.

Readalouds do not grow on trees. You make them. You need a computer and some patience.

You also need two files: an e-book as an EPUB file, and the audiobook of the same book. And you need a helper to join the two. It is called [Storyteller](https://storyteller-platform.dev/), and like me, it is free software. Storyteller listens to the whole audiobook and reads the whole book. Then it notes, for every single sentence, when it is spoken. The result is a new EPUB with the narration inside. Copy it to your phone, and I do the rest.

This book was made that way. Someone wrote the text, an AI voice read it aloud, and a program like Storyteller matched the two. If you tap a sentence and hear exactly that sentence, it worked. If you hear something else, please blame the voice.

**Try it.** Tap any sentence on this page. I start reading there. Now tap the sentence I am speaking. I stop, even in the middle of a word. Tap it again and I go on.

## 7. Your library

My library is sorted by author. Tap an author to see the books. Series come first, in the right order. Then all the other books.

I look for books in Documents, in the music folder and on memory cards, and in all the folders inside them. Or you choose the folders yourself: pull down the menu of the library and choose "Book folders". You can add one folder or several. E-books and audiobooks may be in different folders.

An audiobook is an M4B file, an MP3 file, or a folder of MP3 files. In a folder, each file is a chapter, in the order of the file names. So number your files. I count properly: chapter ten comes after chapter nine, not after chapter one. Yes, I am showing off.

If several MP3 audiobooks share one folder, I tell them apart by their album tag.

But I can only be as tidy as your files. Here are three habits that help.

First, sort names. A good e-book has "Dickens, Charles" somewhere inside, as the name to sort by. Then Charles Dickens appears under the letter D, where he belongs. Without a sort name, he may end up under C, next to Charlotte Brontë, and neither of them will enjoy it.

Second, series. If the book says it is part two of a series, I put it after part one. Audiobooks can say the same with the tags "series" and "series part".

Third, twins. Put an audiobook next to its e-book, in the same folder, and give it the same name. Then I know they belong together. The audiobook gets the title, the author, the series and the cover of the e-book. And when you change from one to the other, I offer to take you to about the same place.

Below is an example. I will not read it aloud. Nobody wants to hear a fish pronounce file names.

[NOT NARRATED]
```
Documents/
└── Books/
    └── Dickens, Charles/
        ├── A Christmas Carol.epub
        ├── A Christmas Carol.mp3        (paired with the EPUB above)
        ├── Oliver Twist.epub
        └── Oliver Twist/                (paired too: a folder named like the EPUB)
            ├── 01 Chapter one.mp3
            ├── 02 Chapter two.mp3
            └── 03 Chapter three.mp3
Music/
└── Audiobooks/
    ├── Treasure Island.m4b              (one file: one audiobook)
    └── Moby Dick/                       (a folder of MP3 files: one audiobook)
        ├── 01 Loomings.mp3
        ├── 02 The Carpet-Bag.mp3
        └── 03 The Spouter-Inn.mp3
```
[/NOT NARRATED]

**Try it.** Look at the example. Find the two audiobooks that have an e-book twin of the same name. Then think of your own folders, and sigh quietly.

## 8. Settings and credits

Nearly done. Pull down the menu and choose "Settings". Here is everything you can change.

"Text" sets size, line spacing, paragraph spacing and font. It also sets one or two columns for when you hold the phone sideways.

"Playback and timer" sets how fast I speak, and the sleep timer from chapter five.

"Button bar" sets how big my buttons are, and whether they sit at the top or at the bottom.

"Dictionary" you know already.

"Orientation" decides whether I turn with the phone or stay as I am.

"Colour mode" is light or dark.

**Try it.** Open the settings and switch the colour mode. Dark is for the night. Light is for the day. Switching back and forth is for people who cannot decide.
