# Welcome to Bookfish

*The greatest book ever written about this app. Also the only one.*

## 1. One place for books

Hello. I am Bookfish. I am a fish, and I live for books. Most fish live for worms. I have better taste.

For centuries, humanity has faced a terrible choice. Read the book, or listen to the book. I end the suffering. I keep three kinds of books in one place:

E-books are for your eyes. Audiobooks are for your ears. And readalouds are for both at once. A readaloud is an e-book that knows exactly which sentence is being spoken. This little book is one.

So read on the sofa. Then the dishes call, as dishes do. Tap a sentence, and I read aloud from exactly that sentence. Your hands wash the dishes. Your ears stay in the story. I never lose your place. I have the memory of an elephant, which is unusual for a fish.

**Try it.** Tap any sentence on this page. I start reading there. Now tap the sentence I am speaking. I stop, even in the middle of a word, and I am not offended. Tap it again and I go on.

## 2. Tap and hold

All my controls fit into one rule. I am very proud of it. Here it comes.

A tap turns the page or starts playback. A long press opens another view.

That is all. Tap the left or right edge of the page to turn it. You can also swipe. Tap a sentence in the middle and I read from there. Tap the sentence I am speaking and I pause.

A long press means: press and hold. It shows you something on its own page. A picture. A footnote. A table. And here is the secret code: blue means "press and hold me". Whenever you see blue text, hold your thumb on it and wait for the miracle.

## 3. Finding your way

A book is a big place. Here is how you never get lost.

Look at the page number. If the book knows the pages of its printed edition, I show those. If not, I estimate them, and I put a small wavy equals sign in front. It means "about". Here is an example.

[NOT NARRATED]
```
≈143/380
```
[/NOT NARRATED]

That is page 143 of about 380. Without the wavy sign, the numbers are the real ones from the printed book. I am an honest fish. Either way, the page numbers stay the same at any text size.

Now find the page number. It sits at the right end of my button bar, the row of small buttons at the top or the bottom of the screen. If you hold the phone sideways, it stands at the edge of the page, turned on its side. Tap it. This opens my flip view: nine small pages, with yours in the middle. Swipe to move through the book. Or tap beside the pages. Or drag the bar below, and fly from the first page to the last. Tap a page to read on there. Close the view, and you are back where you were, as if nothing had happened. "Go to page" lets you type a page number.

One small thing. While my playback buttons are on the screen, the page number is hidden. So there is nothing to tap. Pause me and turn the page, and it comes back.

Everything else lives in the pull-down menu. Pull the page down from the top and choose.

"Contents" lists the chapters.

"Find and history" searches the book. Type a few words, in any order, and I show every paragraph that contains all of them. Type nothing, and the same page shows your history: where you have read and listened. Tap an entry to go there.

"Bookmarks" lists your bookmarks and notes. To make one, press and hold a word. Buttons appear. One makes a bookmark. One makes a note. Not sure which button is which? Hold one down, and it tells you its name.

**Try it.** Three steps. If I am reading aloud, pause me and turn the page first. Then tap the page number, look around, and close the flip view again. Then pull down the menu, choose "Find and history" and search for the word "goldfish". Then press and hold any word on this page and set a bookmark. I suggest the word "glory".

## 4. Words

Some words deserve special attention. Which ones? You decide. Press and hold any word you like, anywhere on the page. This selects it, and two handles appear. Drag the handles to select more.

Now the buttons. Copy it. Bookmark it. Write a note about it. Or look it up in the dictionary.

The dictionary translates a word or a whole phrase, right on your phone, without any network. It even works at the bottom of the sea. It also recognises English words in disguise. Select "went", and it finds "go".

There is one catch. The dictionary uses word lists from dict.cc. They are free for personal use. But dict.cc does not allow me to hand them out. So I arrive without one, and you get your own. It takes four steps.

One. Open the dictionary settings: pull down the menu, choose "Settings", then "Dictionary". If no word list is on the phone, you find a link to dict.cc there.

Two. On the dict.cc page, request the word list for your pair of languages.

Three. Put the file you get into the Documents folder of your phone.

Four. Go back to the dictionary settings. The file is listed there. Tap "Import" and wait a moment.

Done. You may delete the file afterwards. I have swallowed everything I need.

You can keep several word lists on the phone, one for each pair of languages. For example, one for English and German, and one for French and German. The link to dict.cc only shows while you have no list yet. So for each further list, visit dict.cc in your browser and repeat steps two to four. The language of the book decides which word list is used. So for this book, you need a list with English in it. In the dictionary settings, you choose the language to translate into.

**Try it.** Press and hold the word "miracle". Drag a handle and watch the selection grow. Then tap the copy button.

## 5. Your library

My library is sorted by author. Tap an author to see the books. Series come first, in the right order. Then all the other books.

But I can only be as tidy as your files. Here are four habits that help.

First, sort names. A good e-book says "Dickens, Charles" somewhere inside, as the name to sort by. Then Charles Dickens lands under the letter D, where he belongs. Without it, he may end up under C, next to Charlotte Brontë, and neither of them will enjoy it.

Second, series. If the book says it is part two of a series, I put it after part one. Audiobooks can say the same with the tags "series" and "series part".

Third, twins. Give an audiobook the same name as the e-book next to it. Then I know they belong together. The audiobook gets the title, the author, the series and the cover of the book. And when you switch from one to the other, I offer to take you to about the same place.

Fourth, folders. A folder full of MP3 files, with no e-book in it, is one audiobook. Each file is a chapter, in the order of the file names. So number your files. I count properly: chapter ten comes after chapter nine, not after chapter one. Some apps cannot do that. I can. I am showing off.

I look for books in Documents and on memory cards. You can choose another folder in the pull-down menu of the library.

## 6. Make your own readaloud

First, a closer look at what a readaloud is. It is an e-book with the audiobook built in. Every printed sentence is matched with the same sentence in the recording. So at every moment, I know which sentence is being spoken. I mark it while it plays. And I can start at any sentence you tap.

Readalouds do not grow on trees. You make them. It is easier than it sounds.

You need two things: an e-book as an EPUB file, and the audiobook of the same book. Then you need a helper to join the two. It is called [Storyteller](https://storyteller-platform.dev/), and like me, it is free software. Storyteller listens to the whole audiobook and reads the whole book. Then it notes, for every single sentence, when it is spoken. Out comes a new EPUB with the narration inside. Copy it to your phone, and I do the rest.

This very book was made that way. Someone wrote the text. An AI voice read it aloud. Storyteller matched the two, sentence by sentence. If you tap a sentence and hear exactly that sentence, it worked. If you hear something else, please blame the voice.

**Try it.** Tap the last sentence of this chapter. This is it.

## 7. Settings and credits

Nearly done. Pull down the menu and choose "Settings". Here is everything you can adjust.

"Text" sets size, line spacing, paragraph spacing and font, and one or two columns when you hold the phone sideways.

"Playback and timer" sets how fast I speak, and the sleep timer. The timer stops me after fifteen to ninety minutes, or at the end of the chapter. I fade out gently over the last twenty seconds, so I do not wake you.

"Button bar" sets how big my buttons are, and whether they sit at the top or at the bottom. 

"Dictionary" you know already.

"Orientation" decides whether I turn with the phone or stay as I am.

"Colour mode" is light or dark.

**Try it.** Open the settings and switch the colour mode. Dark is for the night. Light is for the day. Switching back and forth is for the undecided.
